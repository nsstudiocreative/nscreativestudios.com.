Why consistency on social media matters

By SophieNovember 9, 20266 min read

Most businesses that give up on social media do it in the first month, right before it starts to work. Consistency matters more than volume, and the real results show up over about three months, not three posts.

The short answer

Posting on a steady schedule gets you more engagement per post and gives people a reason to keep coming back. One great video a month will not do that. A few good posts every week, for months, will. That is why we judge results at the 90-day mark, not after the first week.

What the data says about consistency

Buffer analyzed more than 100,000 accounts over six months and grouped them by how many weeks they posted at least once. Accounts that posted in 20 or more of the 26 weeks got about 5 times more engagement per post than accounts that posted in 4 weeks or fewer. Even moderately consistent accounts got about 4 times more.

A second Buffer study looked at 2.1 million Instagram posts from 102,000 accounts. It found that:

  • 3 to 5 posts a week is the sweet spot for steady growth. Follower growth was more than double that of accounts posting once or twice a week.
  • The biggest jump in reach per post came from moving from 1 to 2 posts a week up to 3 to 5.
  • Weeks with no posts showed below-average growth. Disappearing costs you something.

Consistency is not posting every day

Consistency means a pace you can keep for months. A restaurant that posts three good reels every week for a year will do far better than one that posts every day for two weeks and then goes quiet for a month.

Pick a number you can sustain, even in your busiest season, and protect it. For most local businesses we work with, that is 3 posts a week on Instagram plus stories on busy days.

Why we measure at 90 days

Social media builds on itself. The platforms need time to learn who your content is for, and your audience needs to see you show up more than once before they trust you. Here is roughly what to expect:

What we measure after 90 days

Likes feel good, but they do not pay the bills. At the 90-day review we compare your numbers to where you started and focus on what matters to the business:

  1. Reach: how many people saw your content, and how many of them were new.
  2. Engagement rate: comments, shares and saves compared with how many people saw the post.
  3. Profile visits and website clicks: people moving from curious to interested.
  4. Messages and inquiries: DMs, calls and form submissions that came from social.
  5. Business results: bookings, reservations, event inquiries or orders you can connect to the content.

Then we keep what worked, drop what did not, and plan the next 90 days.

How to stay consistent when you are busy

  • Film in batches. One shoot can give you a month of reels and photos.
  • Use 3 or 4 content themes. For a restaurant, that could be the food, the team, the space and events. Themes make planning faster.
  • Plan a month ahead. A simple calendar removes the daily “what do we post today?” stress.
  • Reuse what works. A good video can become a reel, a story and a short clip for your Google Business Profile.
  • Reply to comments and messages. Being consistent in conversations matters as much as being consistent in posting.
